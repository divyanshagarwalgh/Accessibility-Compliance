The finding that changed the ranking
Accessibility is 46% of all demand found, larger than the Search, Conversion and Scoping clusters combined, and it is the only cluster driven by law rather than fashion.
&apos;color contrast checker&apos; alone runs 12,100 searches per month at $13.08 CPC with a competition index of 2. That is the single best keyword opportunity found anywhere in this research.
&apos;ada compliance checker&apos; is 8,100 per month, &apos;vpat&apos; 5,400, &apos;accessibility checker&apos; 4,400, &apos;wcag compliance&apos; 4,400, &apos;website accessibility checker&apos; 3,600, &apos;wcag 2.2&apos; 2,900, &apos;web accessibility&apos; 2,400.
WebAIM&apos;s 2026 Million study found 95.9% of the top million homepages fail ADA standards, averaging 56.1 WCAG failures each. Almost every site that runs the tool will fail it, which makes qualification near-automatic.
The deadlines run to 2028, so this is not a spike. DOJ extended ADA Title II to 26 April 2027 for large public entities and 26 April 2028 for smaller ones. The EAA has been in force since 28 June 2025 and binds any business selling to EU consumers regardless of where it is based.

[Heading2] Why this ranks first
[ListParagraph] • It is the largest cluster by a wide margin at 44,960 monthly searches on the terms tested, which is 46% of all demand found across the five clusters and larger than Tools 2, 3 and 4 combined.
[ListParagraph] • Demand is statutory, not discretionary. ADA Title II deadlines run to April 2027 and April 2028. The EAA has been enforceable since June 2025. A French court ordered Carrefour to reach full compliance in June 2026, explicitly rejecting a 71% score as insufficient, under a EUR 500 per day penalty. Partial compliance is not a legal position.
[ListParagraph] • The failure rate makes qualification automatic. 95.9% of the top million homepages fail. The tool does not need to find a problem, it needs to quantify one that is already there.
[ListParagraph] • Competition on the biggest keyword is close to zero. A 12,100-per-month term at competition index 2 is anomalous and will not stay that way.
[ListParagraph] • It sells into UI/UX, Development, and Maintenance simultaneously. An accessibility finding is a design fix, a build fix, and an ongoing monitoring commitment all at once.
[ListParagraph] • Webyansh already has topical seed content in the published WCAG 2.2 compliance guide, so the tool lands on an existing internal link surface rather than a cold one.
[Heading2] Build notes
Engine: axe-core (open source, Deque) as the scanning base, run headless in a Vercel function. Do not build a scanner from scratch.
The value layer: axe output is generic and developer-facing. The product value is the translation layer: Webflow Designer steps, jurisdiction mapping, and the generated statement and VPAT. That layer is where the defensibility sits.
Positioning warning: Do not sell or imply an overlay widget. Overlay vendors are being sued, and association would damage credibility with exactly the buyers who care. Position as audit and remediation, never as a one-line fix.
Claim discipline: Never state that the tool guarantees compliance. Automated scanning catches roughly a third of WCAG criteria. Say so plainly on the results page. Honesty here is a differentiator, because competitors overclaim.
Gate: Contrast checker fully open, no email. Full scan report gated. The contrast checker is the traffic engine and gating it would waste the opportunity.

[Heading2] Why consolidation is the right call
The previous roadmap split this into five products. That was a mistake for three reasons. They share almost all their infrastructure, being one crawler, one engine adapter layer, and one report renderer. They share one buyer, being the person responsible for search performance. And five thin tools dilute link equity and internal linking across five weak pages instead of concentrating it on one strong hub with module sub-pages.
The correct structure is a hub at /tools/search-visibility with each module on its own indexable sub-page. That preserves the individual keyword targeting while consolidating authority.
[Heading2] Why it still ranks second, not first
[ListParagraph] • The CPCs are the highest anywhere in this document, at $30 to $67, which signals genuine commercial value.
[ListParagraph] • But the generic end is crowded. HubSpot, Frase, Insites, SUSO and Pixelmojo all ship free AI visibility checkers, with Semrush, Ahrefs and Profound buying the ads above them. At DR 34 the head terms are not winnable head-on.
[ListParagraph] • The winnable positions are the specific ones: page-level rather than domain-level grading, Claude and Google AI Mode inclusion, Webflow-native schema output, and the published Index where no competing dataset exists.
[ListParagraph] • Accessibility outranks it because that cluster is more than three times larger and has almost no incumbent on its biggest keyword.
[Heading2] Build notes
The Index is the long pole: Roughly 400 companies across 5 verticals, 40 prompts each, 5 engines, is about 80,000 calls per quarter. Batch over 10 to 14 days. Budget this before committing.
Claude inclusion: Non-negotiable. Claude carries roughly 18.5% of B2B AI referrals against a 2.66% global referral share, and several well-known free checkers skip it entirely.
Google AI Mode: No official API. Route through the existing DataForSEO SERP access.
Existing asset: Fold the current /tools/website-score tool into this hub or retire it. It is not surfacing in search and duplicates a saturated category.

[Heading2] Why it earns a place

[Heading2] Build notes
Model use: Heuristic scoring should be deterministic where possible (field counts, CTA counts, contrast, fold height). Reserve Claude for the qualitative passes: value proposition clarity and the five-second test.
Calculators first: Ship the sample size and significance calculators in week one. They are trivial to build, need no crawl, and start earning links immediately.
Honesty constraint: A page teardown is a hypothesis, not evidence. Label predicted lifts as estimates with stated assumptions. Overclaiming here is easy to disprove and expensive to the brand.

[Heading2] The honest case
The migration keyword cluster is small. &apos;wordpress to webflow migration&apos; is 50 searches a month and &apos;webflow migration&apos; is 20. On volume alone this tool does not justify itself, and it should not be built as a traffic play.
It justifies itself on four other grounds. The CPCs are $33 to $37, which means every one of those fifty searches is someone with budget and a decision to make. Webyansh already has twelve migration landing pages sitting static, and this tool makes every one of them interactive, which is the cheapest conversion-rate improvement available on the existing site. It captures the top-of-funnel scoping query at 1,900 a month, where the buyer has not chosen a platform yet. And it feeds the contact form&apos;s existing budget bands, so a prospect arrives pre-qualified rather than picking &apos;Not Sure&apos;.
[Heading2] Build notes
Detection: Wappalyzer-style fingerprinting on headers, script sources, meta generators and known asset paths. Well-trodden, low risk.
Estimation discipline: Output a band, never a fixed price. Show the assumptions that produced the band and let the user adjust them. A wrong precise number destroys trust; a well-reasoned range builds it.
Placement: Embed the assessor on all twelve migration pages with the source platform pre-selected, so /migrations/wordpress-to-webflow-migration opens the tool already configured.
Sales use: The generated CMS schema and redirect map become the first deliverable of a paid discovery, which shortens the gap between enquiry and proposal.

[Heading2] Why it ranks last but still gets built
The entry keywords are large but commodity. &apos;website speed test&apos; at 14,800 a month is dominated by PageSpeed Insights, GTmetrix and Pingdom, and a new entrant will not displace them. Treat that volume as a bonus, not a plan.
The reason to build it is different. It is the only tool that creates a standing relationship rather than a single interaction, it makes the Maintenance and Support service line demonstrable instead of abstract, and the publish-diff and integration-health modules are things no general monitoring tool does because they require Webflow-specific knowledge. It also turns every past client into a live account, which is the cheapest pipeline in the business.
[Heading2] Build notes
Free tier: One site, weekly checks. Enough to be useful, not enough to replace a retainer.
Paid tier: Multi-site, daily checks, publish diffs, and integration monitoring. Price it as an add-on to the maintenance retainer rather than a separate product.
Data sources: CrUX API for field data, Lighthouse for lab data, simple HTTP checks for uptime and certificates. Low technical risk throughout.
Sequencing: Build last. It depends on scan logic from Tools 1 and 2, and building those first makes this mostly a scheduler and an alerting layer.

Why it was cut

Standalone generic website grader
Webyansh already runs one at /tools/website-score and it is not surfacing. The category is saturated by HubSpot Website Grader, GTmetrix and IONOS. Absorbed into Tool 3 rather than duplicated.

Crowded with free offerings from HubSpot, Frase, Insites, SUSO and Pixelmojo, with paid placements above them. Folded into Tool 2 as one module among eight.

&apos;website speed test&apos; is 14,800 a month but owned by PageSpeed Insights, GTmetrix and Pingdom. Only viable as the monitoring module inside Tool 5, where the value is the time series rather than the score.
AI content or blog generator
Infinite competition, no data moat, and it undercuts the positioning of an agency selling citation quality and design craft.
Backlink or authority checker
Requires an index at Ahrefs or Semrush scale. Not buildable.
Standalone branding or logo tool
Branding is a small service line with no meaningful tool-intent search demand. Served adequately by the message clarity module in Tool 3.
