What a VPAT actually is

The Voluntary Product Accessibility Template is a document published by the Information Technology Industry Council. Filling one in produces an Accessibility Conformance Report — an ACR. In practice the two words are used interchangeably, and a buyer asking for “your VPAT” means the completed report.

It is voluntary in the sense that nobody forces you to publish one. It stops feeling voluntary the moment a public university, a hospital group or a federal contractor puts it in a procurement checklist, which is where most requests come from.

WCAG edition

The WCAG 2.2 success criteria on their own. The right choice when a private buyer asks for conformance evidence and names no regulation.

Section 508 edition

Adds the US federal chapters. Ask for this one if you sell to a federal agency or to anyone spending federal money.

EU edition

Maps to EN 301 549, the harmonised standard behind the European Accessibility Act. Needed for EU public-sector procurement.

INT edition

All three at once. Longest to complete, and the one to pick if you sell into every market and would rather maintain a single document.

The conformance vocabulary is fixed

A VPAT has four permitted answers per criterion. Using anything else — “mostly”, “in progress”, “95%” — marks the document as amateur to anyone who reads these for a living.

Term What it means What we fill in
Supports The functionality meets the criterion. Only where we tested and found nothing, with a remark naming what was checked.
Partially supports Some functionality does not meet the criterion. Where we found element-level failures. We see failures, never passes, so we cannot claim a majority either way.
Does not support The majority of functionality does not meet the criterion. Where the failure is page-wide. A missing lang attribute is not partially missing.
Not evaluated The criterion has not been assessed. The 43 criteria no scanner can reach. They appear in the table rather than being dropped from it.

There is a fifth value, Not applicable, for criteria that cannot arise in your product — captions for a site with no video, for instance. We never fill that in automatically, because whether your product has video is not something a single page scan can establish.

What the scan can and cannot fill in

  Criteria How they are filled
Evidenced by the scan 12 Pre-filled with a conformance value and a remark naming the failing elements and their count.
Not evaluated 43 Listed with the criterion number and name, marked Not evaluated, waiting for a human.
Total, WCAG 2.2 A and AA 55 Every one appears. None is omitted.

Leaving the untested rows out would be the easier document to produce and the more dangerous one to send. A table containing only the criteria we happen to check reads, to someone working through their own checklist, as a table where everything passed.

How to finish the draft

  1. Scan the pages that represent your product — a template each, not every URL.
  2. Generate the draft and pick the edition your buyer asked for.
  3. Work through the 43 Not evaluated rows. Most need a keyboard, a screen reader and twenty minutes each.
  4. Rewrite our remarks in your own voice. They are accurate, not diplomatic.
  5. Have someone who did not write it read it against the criteria.
  6. Date it, version it, and re-issue when the product changes.

What this does not do

Automated testing evaluates 12 of the 55 WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA success criteria, about 22%. The rest need a person: whether alternative text is meaningful, whether reading order makes sense, whether a keyboard user can finish the task. We will not tell you that you are compliant, and a VPAT produced from a scan alone is not one you should send. This is a starting draft that removes the mechanical half of the work.

Questions

Is a VPAT legally required?

No. It is a voluntary disclosure. What is required is the underlying accessibility, under the ADA, Section 508, the European Accessibility Act and others depending on where you sell. The VPAT is how a buyer checks before committing, so in procurement it behaves like a requirement even though no statute names it.

Can I publish a VPAT that admits failures?

Yes, and it is usually the better document. Buyers expect Partially supports rows; what loses deals is a report that claims full support and falls apart when their own team tests it. A candid report with a remediation date attached is a stronger position than an optimistic one.

How often should it be re-issued?

Whenever the product changes materially, and at least annually. A VPAT carries a date and a version for that reason. On Webflow, a publish can change the rendered markup without anyone editing a template, so tie the review to your release cadence rather than the calendar.

Do you complete VPATs for clients?

Yes. The generator handles the mechanical rows; the manual criteria need a person testing with a keyboard and a screen reader. If you would rather not do that in house, we do the review, complete the document and hand back something you can send.
